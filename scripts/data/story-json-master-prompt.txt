Behavior-specific guardrails:
* Rude/disrespectful tone: do not turn the story into an unrelated cup, sip, toy, or object-compliance story. Show the parent calmly holding the communication boundary, modeling the replacement phrase, and letting the child try again with kind/respectful words. The child must visibly practice the replacement request before the ending.
* Aggression: do not show harm. Show the unsafe impulse being stopped or redirected before anyone is hurt.
* Anxiety and fears: do not force bravery. Let the child choose a small distance or partial participation.
* Food-related issues: avoid pressure, shame, and moralizing. It is acceptable to use a realistic family incentive or first/then structure when the child still makes a small concrete food-related attempt, such as sitting, touching, tasting, or taking one bite.
* Refusal or transition resistance: use a predictable cue, first/then, countdown, save-the-place ritual, or two acceptable choices that both move toward the needed next step.
* Sharing or grabbing: use a clear turn boundary, return cue, timer-like marker, or repair action. Do not reward grabbing by letting the child keep the toy.
* Cleanup: the choice must be between two acceptable ways to complete cleanup, such as dropping the block in or placing it gently. Never let the floor be an acceptable end state when the goal is basket/bin.
* Screen limits: preview the ending, hold the limit, and move into the next concrete activity. The screen must not return because the child protests.
* Separation/dropoff: use a short predictable goodbye ritual and confident return language. Do not stretch the goodbye because the child escalates.
* Perfectionism/frustration intolerance: stay entirely with one concrete problem, such as one drawing or one fallen object. Do not jump to a second activity.
* Public behavior struggles: allow disappointment to remain; do not turn the ending into instant compliance.
* Safety issues: set the safety boundary immediately before offering any choice. Stop, hold, block, carry, or move the child to safety first; never bargain while the child remains unsafe.
